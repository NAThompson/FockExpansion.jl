One remaining correction, at lines 128–129:

“corrections chosen in ker ℓ” is false if it means ξ_n ∈ ker ℓ for
n ≥ 1. Those components are already determined by the next solvability
condition and generally do not vanish. Normalise only ℓ(ξ_0)=1. Write
ξ_n = a_n ξ_0 + v_n, v_n ∈ ker ℓ; solve for v_n with B0 restricted to
ker ℓ (onto range B0), and determine a_n from solvability at step n+1.
The resulting full ξ_n are unique and holomorphic. The step-n statement
about a nonzero coefficient applies for n ≥ 2, not n=1.

Concrete counterexample within the stated hypotheses: m=1, U=D=0,
W=-1, E=0, K=0, and ℓ(x,y)=x. Then
 A0=[0 1; 1 0], σ=-1, ξ_0=(1,-1), β=-5/2, C2=0.
The first two solvability steps give
 ξ_1=(15/8,-35/8),
so ℓ(ξ_1)=15/8 ≠ 0. These values satisfy the recurrence exactly.
Thus ker ℓ can be used to choose the complementary part v_n, not the
whole correction ξ_n. This is a proof-wording repair; the theorem's
normalisation in (b), and the convergence conclusion, survive it.
