Verdict
The interior/exterior matching architecture is sound. Claims (2) and (3)
need substantial qualification. In particular, the actual cusp model does
NOT have m negative and m positive characteristic roots, the nearest-root
Borel-radius assertion is not generally an equality, and a convergent
factorial representation does not follow just from the singularity ranks.
Claim (4) is correct for the ungauged fixed-channel model, but its proposed
extension to the projected cusp model conflates two different projections.

I read FIXED_ENERGY_EXTRACTION.txt, README.txt, cusp_matching.jl, probe.jl,
and exterior_series.jl. I ran a small characteristic-spectrum calculation
using the existing Julia code, without modifying source files. This is a
mathematical review and limited diagnostic, not a convergence certificate.

1. What the implemented exterior is

Claim (1) is correct. cuspexterior constructs local fundamental-matrix
Taylor polynomials B around successive positive radii, transports their
logarithmic derivative, and initializes at finite rmax with Y0+Y1/rmax.
The coefficient singularity at zero guarantees analyticity in |R-r|<r;
this is a guaranteed disk, not necessarily the exact radius of a particular
solution (a removable singularity can allow a larger disk). Matrix
inversion can introduce Riccati poles even where the linear solutions are
regular. Truncated panels also carry numerical truncation error.

The cusp boundary condition is not the ungauged -kappa I-V/(kappa rmax).
It uses a selected invariant subspace of the companion matrix and a
Sylvester correction. Thus it already accounts for the constant cusp
matrices, but retains a finite-rmax asymptotic initialization error.

2. Sensitivity: correct mechanism, incorrect absolute impossibility claim

For an ungauged finite-channel bound state, write schematically
 f=A_- exp(-kappa R) R^{B_-}+A_+ exp(kappa R) R^{B_+}.
At an eigenpair, the selected origin amplitudes have A_+=0. A generic
perturbation transverse to this constraint introduces delta A_+. Relative
to the desired decaying signal, contamination grows like
 delta A_+/A_- times exp(2 kappa R), with additional powers of R.
For kappa=sqrt(5.807448754)=2.4099..., the exponential costs about
2 kappa/log(10)=2.093 digits per unit R.

This is RELATIVE conditioning near the selected decaying solution, not
the absolute norm of the map from origin data to f(R). The absolute
amplification is typically exp(kappa R); the second factor comes from
dividing by a signal of size exp(-kappa R). Nor does every perturbation
excite the growing sector: normalization changes and tangent directions
are exceptions. At an arbitrary fixed E that is not an eigenvalue of the
finite model, a normalized regular solution satisfying all exterior
conditions generally does not exist at all. At the reference helium E,
the finite-model constrained mismatch need not vanish, however accurately
one evaluates its series.

Consequently, replace 'cannot reach large R regardless of resummation'
with 'fixed-accuracy origin data cannot generically deliver fixed relative
accuracy in the bound-state tail at arbitrarily large R.' Arbitrary
precision in both data and evaluation can reach any finite R. Exact data
with exact analytic continuation has no such obstruction. Resummation can
remove evaluation/cancellation difficulties but cannot remove uncertainty
in supplied data. A boundary-aware resummation can impose the missing
selection condition, but then it has added information beyond uncertain
origin coefficients.

For simple cusp roots, contamination of mode i by mode j scales instead
as exp(Re(lambda_j-lambda_i) R), times powers and eigenvector conditioning.
There is no universal 2 kappa for this projected cusp system. The relevant
pair depends on the desired signal and the excited error; the largest
relevant gap controls the worst asymptotic relative error. Non-normality
can matter at finite R. An exact scalar exponential gauge preserves root
differences, but this angularly projected cusp model is not merely such a
scalar shift of the ungauged finite model.

3. Actual cusp roots: a concrete correction

With E=-2.903724377, g=1, the code gives:
 d=0 (m=1): lambda=0.03290471776, 4.43924660254.
 d=2 (m=4): selected four roots approximately
   -0.2342196351, -0.0054140762, 0.0833973611, 0.3563717616;
   other four: 3.9059116096, 4.4677445939, 4.5904176533, 4.7089227852.
All were real in this diagnostic. cusptail selects the m roots with
smallest real parts; it does NOT select all negative roots. Phi can grow
while the restored physical wavefunction decays. Use 'selected slow
subspace' or a physically specified exterior subspace, not 'decaying roots
of Phi'. A numerical spectral gap alone is not a proof that this selection
is exactly equivalent to the required weighted physical L2 condition.

The g=0, E=-4 control is also revealing: W+2E I=0, so lambda=0 has
multiplicity m. For d=2 there are four zero roots and four positive roots.
Repeated roots are therefore present in an actual supplied control, not
just a hypothetical edge case.

4. Formal infinity expansion and resonances

Set P(lambda)=lambda^2 I+2U lambda+T and beta=-mu. For a simple
characteristic root take
 Phi=exp(lambda R) R^beta sum_{n>=0} h_n R^{-n}, P(lambda)h_0=0.
With negative-index coefficients zero, substitution gives
 P(lambda) h_n
 +[(beta-n+1)(2lambda I+2U)+5lambda I+D] h_{n-1}
 +[(beta-n+2)(beta-n+6)I-Lambda] h_{n-2}=0.
If v=h_0 and w is a left null vector of P(lambda), solvability at n=1 gives
 beta=-w(5lambda I+D)v / [w(2lambda I+2U)v].
This explicitly shows how D/R changes the power and the recurrence.
For a simple root the denominator is nonzero. Subsequent coefficients
require solvability and a normalization convention, rather than naive
inversion of the singular P(lambda).

For distinct exponential roots, integer differences between beta values
on DIFFERENT exponential branches do not by themselves force logarithms.
Exponentials separate these branches. Resonance is an issue within a
repeated exponential block: diagonalize/reduce its effective 1/R matrix,
then inspect integer exponent differences and compatibility conditions.
Jordan structure can produce logs; defective leading blocks can require
more general formal forms, including ramified exponential corrections.
Repeated roots alone do not force logs. The zero block in the g=0 control
must be treated explicitly rather than passed through a simple-root theorem.

Zero is regular singular (the indicial matrix is n(n+4)I-Lambda).
Infinity has a first-order formulation with coefficients A0+A1/R+A2/R^2,
so it has Poincare rank at most one in that formulation. This classification
alone is weaker than a nonresonant, unramified rank-one normal-form theorem.

5. Borel radius and factorial convergence

After factoring the exponential and power, the generic simple-root formal
series is of Gevrey-1 type. With the convention exp(-Rt) for the Borel
Laplace kernel, candidate singularities relative to branch i occur at
 t=lambda_i-lambda_j.
These are candidate locations, not necessarily actual singularities of
every solution/component. Coupling or connection coefficients can vanish;
series can terminate. Thus 'finite radius equals the distance to the
nearest other root' is false without a nonvanishing-singularity hypothesis.
In suitable simple-root settings the nearest candidate gives a guaranteed
local analyticity scale, and equality requires an actual obstruction.
One must specify which Borel or Borel-Leroy transform is used for R^beta.
Repeated blocks cannot be handled by declaring their radius to be zero.

An especially elementary exception is the exact g=0 ground-state solution:
Phi=1 in the constant angular channel at E=-4. Its inverse-power correction
series terminates although other characteristic roots are at finite distance.

A finite Borel Taylor disk is insufficient to prove a single convergent
inverse-factorial series to infinity. A typical construction sets
 t=-a log(1-u), a>0, in a Laplace integral. One must establish analyticity
of the transformed Borel function throughout |u|<1, choose branches and
contours, and control its growth at the boundary, especially u=1.
Mapped singularities u_j=1-exp(-t_j/a) can obstruct that disk. A positive
real Borel singularity lies inside it for every positive a and calls for
contour choices or a different representation. Growth bounds then set an
abscissa of convergence Re(R)>sigma, with scaling/shift conventions.
Existence for some sufficiently large R is different from validity down
to any prescribed R0>0. A local coefficient conversion does not establish
these global analytic hypotheses or select a lateral/Stokes prescription.

There is a further selection issue: the slowest decreasing solution can
be modified by a faster decreasing solution without changing its complete
inverse-power asymptotic expansion. Thus an algebraic recurrence alone
does not uniquely specify each exterior column. Ambiguities confined to
the selected subspace may leave its logarithmic derivative unchanged,
but that must be demonstrated; ambiguities involving excluded branches
change the boundary condition. This matters for a numerical test of (3).

Useful primary-source context, not a theorem for these matrices:
 Weniger, 'Summation of Divergent Power Series by Means of Factorial Series',
 https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.0466
 Nemes, 'On the Borel summability of WKB solutions of certain
 Schrodinger-type differential equations', https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13367
These discuss factorial summation and, in the latter setting, half-plane
convergence under specific hypotheses. They do not establish the proposed
unconditional assertion for this coupled cusp system.

6. Laplace transform: derive it before calling it equivalent

For F(s)=integral_0^infinity exp(-sR) Phi(R) dR, multiply the ODE by R^2
before transforming. Assuming the endpoint terms and convergence justify
the calculation, direct differentiation gives
 P(s) F''(s)+[-s I+4U-D] F'(s)-[3I+Lambda] F(s)=0.
For an exterior-only transform from R0>0 there are additional entire
forcing terms containing the data at R0. A transform from zero must also
encode which origin solution is meant and its admissible endpoint behavior.

At a simple zero of det P(s), P(s)^{-1} has at most a simple pole. The
2m-dimensional companion system for (F,F') consequently has a regular
singularity there. At infinity its second-order coefficients have the
regular-singular orders O(1/s) and O(1/s^2); use (F,sF') to see this in a
first-order formulation. Infinity is another regular singular point.
Repeated determinant roots need examination of the POLE ORDER OF THE
MATRIX INVERSE: multiplicity alone does not decide Fuchsianity. For example,
the ungauged P=(s^2-kappa^2)I has repeated determinant roots but simple
matrix-inverse poles. Defective cases need separate reduction.

So the transform offers a promising Fuchsian description under the right
hypotheses, with finite candidate singularities at characteristic roots
and an additional point at infinity. It is not automatically equivalent
to a chosen convergent factorial basis with the correct exterior selection.
The shifted Borel transform of a power-normalized asymptotic branch also
need not satisfy this same homogeneous equation without extra terms.

7. Threshold physics and the cusp gauge

In the UNGAUGED fixed hyperspherical projection, the limiting equation is
f''+5f'/R+2E f=0, so every exterior exponential rate is kappa=sqrt(-2E).
The finite V/R and Lambda/R^2 matrices cannot generate the nonzero ionic
threshold energy at infinity. This part of claim (4) is correct.

For an infinitely heavy nucleus and ground ionic channel, the outer-electron
rate is sqrt(2(E_He+ - E))=sqrt(-2(E+2)), about 1.3444 here. The expression
sqrt(2(E+2)) in the request has the WRONG SIGN: E+2 is negative.
There is also a Coulomb power prefactor, and the one-electron escape sector
is not a uniform fixed-angle asymptotic statement on the entire hypersphere.
Its angular width shrinks like 1/R because the ionic electron remains at
bounded distance. Fixed polynomial harmonics therefore need increasing
resolution. Threshold-adapted hyperspherical channels have a different,
R-dependent angular structure; see the primary calculations in
 https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.52.3362
 https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.33.2212

The finite cusp model is NOT just the ungauged finite model rewritten.
Projection after the exact scalar angular gauge and gauging after finite
projection do not commute. In particular, P_m exp(R u(Omega)) P_m is not
exp(R P_m u P_m), and projected W is not generally U^2. Its characteristic
roots are genuinely different and its restored basis already has strongly
R-dependent angular weights. The scalar gauge is
 S=-Z(r1+r2)+(g/2)r12.
In the single-escape sector it supplies exp((-Z+g/2)r2), hence exp(-1.5 r2)
for helium. This already builds in a useful part of the localization and
outer decay; it is exact for independent electrons when g=0, as the
constant-Phi control demonstrates.

It does not by itself enforce the interacting ionic rate 1.3444. Phi must
supply the remaining behavior, and a finite cusp projection still needs
an independent asymptotic error analysis. Therefore 'every cusp channel
has the gauge-shifted analog of kappa' is not justified, while 'fixed
hyperspherical channels necessarily need more channels' should be limited
to the plain fixed harmonic ansatz, not every gauge-adapted representation.
For actual physical decay tests restore the scalar angular gauge, rather
than interpreting exp(RU) as an exact finite-dimensional reconstruction.

8. Recommended next steps / alternative exterior

For this finite rational matrix ODE, pursue the factorial construction as
a conditional theorem: establish the selected invariant subspace, handle
repeated blocks, derive the Borel equation and its continuation, then prove
an explicit convergence half-plane and an error bound. A successful
partial-sum test is evidence, not a substitute for those hypotheses.
Compare the resulting boundary MAP, not just individual columns, against
panels while independently varying rmax, panel order, factorial order,
precision, and branch/scaling choices. Include g=0 and small-channel cases.

A less ambitious but rigorous route is an exterior Volterra/Jost integral
equation around a solvable Coulomb reference, with a convergent Neumann
series at sufficiently large matching radius. In the ungauged model,
diagonalize symmetric V and use scalar Whittaker/Coulomb solutions for the
-2E+2V/R reference; the centrifugal/coupling remainder is O(R^-2).
An appropriate exponentially weighted Green operator can give a computable
contraction bound for sufficiently large R0. For the cusp model, use its
selected constant-coefficient splitting and include 1/R powers in the
reference; verify the corresponding weighted operator estimates.
This removes an approximate finite endpoint once proved, even if panels
are still convenient to continue inward.

For full helium rather than a fixed matrix model, ionic-channel/Dyson-orbital
or R-dependent hyperspherical adiabatic exteriors, including Coulomb and
polarization corrections and channel coupling, directly address the correct
threshold. Interior Fock data matched to such an exterior is a stronger
physical proposal than declaring the fixed-channel two-series form global.

A defensible revised claim is: the finite-channel ODE admits a global
analytic exterior subspace on the positive axis, subject to the physical
boundary condition; inverse-factorial expansions are a promising possible
representation on a provable half-plane, not yet an established two-series
closed form for this model, and not a full-helium convergence result.
