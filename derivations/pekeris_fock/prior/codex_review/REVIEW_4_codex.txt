Review of REQUEST_4_twoseries.txt — 2026-10-19

Verdict

This is a substantial improvement over the previous matching experiment:
the Fock recurrence, exterior coefficients, determinant, and amplitude solve
are genuinely evaluated in BigFloat. I found no Float64 bottleneck that
would force all the reported differences to stop at approximately 1e-16.
The supplied d=6 experiments support the carefully qualified statement
"observed energy self-consistency at the approximately 1e-28 absolute level
under the five tested changes, for the fixed supplied matrices." They do
not certify a 1e-28 error bound, a 1e-41 total eigenvalue error, or convergence
of the exterior factorial series. The root finder and reporting need some
small but important improvements before making stronger claims.

Scope: I read twoseries.jl, exterior_lib.jl, all three twoseries logs and the
CSV, REVIEW_3, and the underlying cusp_matching.jl implementation, including
model construction and panel boundary selection. I also prepared a local
runtime audit, audit4.jl; its results are recorded below. I did not run the
original script: its top-level driver appends to ../exterior_factorial/
twoseries.csv. No source changes are proposed or made in that directory.

1. Precision chain

cuspcoeff_big (lines 7–34) faithfully copies the original recurrence,
including resonant logarithms and independent regular seed columns. It
constructs T from promoted W and BigFloat E, rather than promoting a
Float64 W+2E expression. fock_FD evaluates both F and F' with BigFloat
powers/logarithms. Orders include every retained seed. There is no use of
the old Float64 evalseries on this path.

riccati_coeffs_bigE (lines 63–85) uses Float64(E) ONLY to obtain a starting
solvent from cusptail. It then constructs T at the actual BigFloat E and
Newton-solves Y0^2+2 U Y0+T=0 with the correct Frechet derivative
 Delta -> (Y0+2U) Delta + Delta Y0.
Consequently the initial eigensolver does not impose a Float64 floor if
Newton converges to the intended solvent. Higher Riccati coefficients are
computed at rprec, using the correct Sylvester recurrence. Add an explicit
post-loop residual check and failure if 12 iterations are insufficient;
currently exhaustion silently proceeds. Check solvent branch/spectral gap
and Sylvester conditioning as well as residual for a reusable solver.

The Stirling table contains exact BigInt integers. factorial_coeffs uses
the precision of the coefficient entries, hence 1024 bits in the base
runs. yfactorial_big sums in that precision and avoids the Float64-returning
helpers in exterior_lib. R* is an exactly representable integer here.
omega is a Float64 number promoted to BigFloat, so the expansion uses that
fixed binary value of omega, not an exact decimal .284. This is harmless
for a convergent representation whose limit is independent of omega;
it can affect finite truncation and belongs in the convergence checks.

match executes the determinant, column norms and least-squares null solve
at prec=384. The exterior sum is returned at high precision and converted
by BigFloat.(...) inside the 384-bit block. Even if the exterior retains
extra entry bits in an intermediate, its matrix product and subsequent
operations are governed by the 384-bit block. There is no downcast to
Float64 in the matching function.

Important qualification: secant_root itself is called OUTSIDE every
setprecision block (line 146). In an ordinary fresh Julia process its
iterates, secant updates and returned slope are at the ambient 256 bits,
not 384 bits. The precision variant changes the matching evaluations to
512/1536 bits but leaves this root arithmetic unchanged. Approximately
77 decimal digits at 256 bits are ample for the observed 1e-28 comparisons;
this is not an explanation for a 1e-28 artificial floor. Nevertheless,
"the entire calculation is 384-bit" is inaccurate. Wrap the driver/root
solve and variant corrections in an explicit precision block and record
precision(Er). Float64 h=1e-9 and tol=1e-24 are merely fixed initial step
and stopping parameters, not rounding of each iterate to Float64.

The displayed energies/a21 and the CSV values are explicitly Float64.
Crucially, differences are formed BEFORE casting, so their nonzero tiny
values are not manufactured by subtracting rounded doubles. However the
CSV cannot reconstruct the high-precision roots, slopes or residuals.
Save full decimal roots, amplitudes, f, f_E and residual-based corrections.

2. Frozen model versus intended angular model

The supplied U,D,W were assembled in Float64, using Float64 quadrature,
normalizations and algebra. Their promotion defines a perfectly legitimate
fixed matrix ODE to arbitrary numerical precision. Both interior and
exterior use these SAME entries, and form their energy dependence in
BigFloat, so this shared input does not fake agreement for that fixed ODE.
It does mean every variant is blind to the common model-construction
error. Arbitrary-precision integration cannot establish 28 digits of the
ideal projected operator, the exact angular integrals, or the untruncated
physical problem. Define the finite model explicitly as the frozen binary
matrices if making a high-precision claim about it; otherwise rebuild the
matrices and quadrature at increasing precision/order independently.

For the supplied defaults Z=2, g=1, the Float64 scalar products in the a21
conversion are exact (including g^2/8=1/8), and big(pi) supplies a
high-precision pi. This formula has no observed Float64 floor here. For
arbitrary Z/g, products such as b.Z*b.g and b.g^2/8 would be evaluated in
Float64 before meeting BigFloat, so promote those scalars first in a
general-purpose implementation.

3. What the stability tests establish

The d=6 CSV gives absolute dE:
 R*+1, with Fock order also +25:   -4.5500972275e-36
 K+60:                           -3.6240386289e-31
 omega*1.25:                     -3.0727175718e-29
 Fock+30:                        -2.9080817655e-41
 precision 384/1024 -> 512/1536:  -2.9080817655e-41.
The corresponding largest |delta a21| is 2.1717075509e-31. Thus "roughly
1e-28 observed absolute self-consistency" is a fair conservative numerical
description. Strictly, "all changes <=3e-29" rounds the largest result
down: it is 3.073e-29. This d=6 conclusion does not extend to d=2 or d=4;
their omega changes move E by about 5.79e-20 and 3.59e-22 respectively.

These are five finite comparisons sharing a recurrence and frozen model,
not five independent error bounds. One K increment and one omega change
cannot exclude a shared truncation bias, long transient or eventual
divergence. Several increasing K values at multiple omega, plus a jointly
strengthened run, would make the numerical convergence case stronger.
Compare exterior matrices/term blocks and full normalized amplitude
vectors too: an eigenvalue can be insensitive to boundary-map errors in
other directions. A matrix Riccati ODE residual is another useful check,
but also needs conditioning information to become an error estimate.

Each variant uses only ONE correction Ev=Er-f_variant(Er)/slope_base
(lines 153–158). Column-normalized determinants have the same zeros but
their slopes can change under changes of R and truncation. In particular,
there is no radius-invariance theorem for their normalized slopes. Tiny
raw residuals alone do not bound errors in energy; divide by that
variant's checked nonzero derivative. Solve each variant independently
or iterate using its own derivative until its estimated residual error
is much smaller than the quoted difference. This is especially important
for the R* comparison. At a simple root the arbitrary normalization does
not obstruct differentiating f on the real energy line.

The nearly identical Fock and precision shifts are naturally explained
by the common remaining base-root error. The default secant tolerance
is 1e-24 in step size, and no residual/slope test is enforced. Superlinear
convergence can overshoot this to a remaining correction of approximately
1e-41, but that must be measured, not inferred as a total accuracy bound.
The variants' extra correction then removes this error in both cases.
At most call 1e-41 an estimated root-iteration correction for the truncated
base determinant after checking f/f_E. Its exterior truncation uncertainty
is a separate, much larger issue, as the K/omega variations demonstrate.

Check the returned slope for finiteness: if the last step rounds to zero,
line 111 divides by zero. Also report failure on maxit exhaustion, and
verify simple-root separation/conditioning. The constrained least-squares
amplitude solve is correct at a simple root with a[1] nonzero; report its
normalized residual and conditioning, and check all components rather
than a21 alone. A fixed amplitude normalization is part of the definition.

4. Is it the panels' exterior branch?

Yes, at the level of asymptotic branch selection. cusptail explicitly
selects the m pencil eigenvalues with smallest real parts, checks a gap
above 0.1, and forms their graph Y0. Both methods invoke that same
routine. Newton refinement should preserve that branch locally; verify
its invariant-subspace spectrum against the separated pencil groups.
"Slow" means this entire m-dimensional group, not just modes with negative
real exponent. Some selected exponents can be positive in the cusp gauge.

The panel boundary itself is NOT exactly the factorial boundary: panels
start at rmax=24 with Y0+Y1/rmax and integrate inward with finite Taylor
panels in Float64. The factorial construction targets the all-orders
asymptotic boundary at infinity. They implement the same intended
subdominant selection, with different finite approximations. Agreement
in E and a21 at roughly 1e-16 is a useful cross-check of this interpretation,
not a proof that the panel boundary or root is correct to every double
precision digit. Panel matching also selects an eigenvalue of D/F-Yo;
the determinant can have other roots. Establish isolation of the chosen
nearby root rather than identifying every determinant zero with the
panel-selected state.

5. What remains before the convergent-series assertion

The determinant formulation is sound. For the frozen ODE let F be the
regular origin frame with the stated logarithmic seed normalization and
G the selected exterior frame. The invariant matching condition is
 det [F G; F' G']=0.
Where the top block of G is invertible, it is equivalent to
 det(F'-Y F)=0.
Exact zeros are independent of matching radius. The Y chart must remain
valid at the chosen radius; use the full frame/exterior pairing if it
fails. Frame determinant prefactors need not be constant with radius.

The regular generalized Frobenius/Fock construction has no other finite
ODE coefficient singularity beyond R=0. Its logarithms require a chosen
sheet; do not call it an entire power series at zero. Supply a convergence
statement for that regular frame, including local uniformity in E near
the root and validity of termwise differentiation.

The missing major theorem is still convergence of the nonlinear Y-chart
inverse factorial expansion at these radii, to the selected exterior
boundary map. Formal recurrence plus exact Stirling conversion plus
finite stability do not prove it. Establish transformed-Borel analyticity
and growth (or another appropriate factorial-series theorem), a sufficient
half-plane of convergence for the chosen omega, and identification of
its sum with the selected asymptotic solution. Account for chart poles;
a bounded pole scan does not locate all poles or prove the abscissa.
Convergence must hold locally uniformly in E near the desired root for
a clean passage from truncated determinants to the exact isolated root.
The earlier linear Plucker Borel argument is a possible alternative
route; it does not automatically prove the Y-chart claim.

Finally specify the frozen matrices, channel set, regular seed convention,
exterior branch, admissible matching radius, and which isolated root is
called E. With the convergence and identification hypotheses stated,
"two convergent series and one determinant define E of the finite-channel
model" is mathematically legitimate. At present the accurate unconditional
wording is: "a BigFloat two-series matching computation, numerically
consistent with the panel root and internally stable at about 1e-28 in
energy for the tested d=6 variations; convergence of the exterior
factorial representation remains to be established."

6. Independent runtime audit

I ran audit4.jl with Julia --startup-file=no, loading the function definitions
without the output-writing driver. The completed run (audit4.log) confirmed:

* Ambient/root precision is 256 bits; solvent coefficients have 1024 bits.
* At the supplied approximate energies, solvent residual norms for d=2,4,6
  were approximately 8.08e-309, 2.62e-308 and 5.50e-308 respectively.
  These are arithmetic-floor residuals, not certified forward-error bounds.
* Spectra of the refined solvents (converted to Float64 only for this branch
  diagnostic) agree with the slow pencil group. For d=6 the selected range
  is [-.280964,.574732], with the fast group starting at 3.377559.
* Reproducing the d=2 base solve gave f=-4.1715103488e-48,
  slope=-6.1322612648e-4, and f/slope=+6.8025646147e-45.
  Its negative is exactly the supplied precision-variant shift. This
  directly supports the remaining-base-iteration-error interpretation.
* For the d=2 R*=5 variant, the derivative at the corrected energy divided
  by the base slope is 0.1498098621. Its remaining f/f_E correction is
  +4.4863876920e-22, whereas the reported one-step dE is -7.9053507176e-23.
  Thus this radius test demonstrably does not give a fully solved variant
  root, despite its tiny raw determinant residual. This does not threaten
  the panel-level agreement, but it must be corrected before interpreting
  all variant shifts as actual root differences.

The additional completed audit4_d6.jl run (audit4_d6.log) estimated the
energy derivatives at the supplied Float64 panel energy using a BigFloat
forward difference h=1e-20. It found
 base R*=10 slope:  -4.6845268511e-54
 R*=11 slope:       -5.8552075950e-59
 ratio:              1.2499037322e-5.
Thus the reported radius step understates the estimated root displacement
by about 80,006. Using this ratio, its remaining energy correction is
approximately -3.6403e-31, rather than an energy error on the scale of its
raw residual 2.1e-89. This is a local derivative estimate near the root,
not a new independently converged variant solve or certified bound. It
nevertheless remains smaller than the omega-induced shift 3.0727e-29,
so this check does not overturn the qualified 1e-28 self-consistency
assessment. It strongly confirms why the variant-slope check is necessary.
