File reviewed: ../proof/EXTERIOR_CONVERGENCE.md (line numbers below).

Verdict

The central linear Borel/factorial argument works: under the separated
m-versus-m spectral split, the canonically normalized compound solution
has an absolutely convergent factorial representation throughout Re R>0.
Defective slow eigenvalues do not obstruct this argument. This is a real
advance over the earlier numerical evidence.

The draft is not correct verbatim. Repair the Abel factor, the proof that
the slow wedge belongs to the compound slow line, parameter uniformity
and normalization, the quantifier on truncated roots, and the Y-chart
hypothesis. These repairs preserve the two-convergent-series conclusion
locally near any gap point and simple matching zero. The theorem proves
a convergence half-plane Re R>0, not that its abscissa is exactly zero.

I did not modify ../proof or ../exterior_factorial. This is a mathematical
review, not a new numerical validation of the supplied gap or eigenvalue.

1. Simplicity: yes, including defective blocks (lines 50–53, 93–98)

The triangularization proof is correct. Each different m-element index
set replaces r>=1 slow roots by r fast roots and raises the real part of
the sum by at least r*g. There is just one all-slow index set, even when
individual slow roots repeat. Thus sigma has algebraic multiplicity one.
Equivalently, the top exterior power of the generalized slow spectral
space is one-dimensional; its nilpotent contribution vanishes.

Be explicit that w is a complex-linear left eigenvector (row vector),
and P=xi0*w/(w*xi0). For complex data, use compatible left/right spectral
vectors rather than silently treating a transpose as a Hermitian adjoint.
A real gap split is conjugation invariant, so sigma and beta are real at
real E, although individual roots may be nonreal.

The phrase "g=0, E=-4 control ... is covered" is ambiguous: if g means
the spectral gap defined here, it contradicts (G). If it means the model's
coupling constant, rename it, and explicitly retain a positive spectral
gap for that control. A repeated slow zero alone says nothing about the
location of the fast roots.

2. Formal recurrence and Fuchsian Borel equation: correct

The recurrence and beta solvability condition are correct. Equation (2)
is indeed satisfied by the formal Borel series; no prior convergence or
actual Laplace transform is needed for this formal coefficient identity.
The low-order boundary identities needed to recover the original equation
are, explicitly,

 B0*xi0=0,
 B0*h(0)+B1*xi0=0,
 B0*h'(0)+(I+B1)*h(0)+C2*xi0=0.

Equation (2) alone has other solutions; retaining these identities and
the prescribed formal germ is essential to identification.

For z=(h,h'), the residue at zero is

 Q = [ 0       0 ; -P*C2    -P*(2I+B1) ].

Its rank is exactly one: P*B1*P=0 by the definition of beta, whereas
P*(2I+B1)*P=2P. In fact Q^2=-2Q, so its eigenvalues are -2 and 0. This
also gives a short convergence justification: write t*z'=M(t,E)*z,
M(0,E)=Q(E). The coefficient equations involve nI-Q for n>=1; these
are invertible and their inverses are O(1/n). The ordinary Fuchsian
majorant argument gives convergence of the prescribed formal series,
uniformly on smaller disks and on compact local parameter sets. There
is no hidden irregular singularity at zero, even if other compound
blocks are defective. Other finite Borel singularities can be irregular;
that does not affect this argument.

Local convergence plus continuation in the disk with its center removed,
and removability of the canonical germ at the center, gives radius at
least min_{sigma'!=sigma}|sigma-sigma'| >= g. The claim is a lower bound;
some candidate singularities can be removable for this particular h.

The global continuation wording at lines 58–60/130 should specify a
branch anchored at the germ at zero. Standard ODE continuation directly
applies along paths avoiding Sigma UNION {0}; it does not automatically
say that arbitrary branches obtained by winding around Sigma are regular
when returned to zero. A sufficient and clean statement for this proof is:
"The germ h extends single-valuedly and holomorphically to Re t>-g."
Here zero is removable for the selected germ and there are no other
singularities in the half-plane. More global claims are unnecessary.

3. Growth lemma: correct, with minor details to add

The shear gives precisely O(|t|^(-1/2))+O(|t|^(-1)) coefficients.
Horizontal integration in a fixed strip produces exp(C sqrt(|t|)) times
a power, rather than exp(C|t|). Take T0>theta as well as T0>2||B0||,
and use the principal square root on the right half-plane. The compact
base-set bound must include h' as well as h. Analyticity supplies both.
The shear bounds h' as well, and equation (2) bounds h'' for large t.
Consequently all boundary terms in the later integrations by parts vanish.

For parameter-uniform constants, first choose a local holomorphic
normalization and a common spectral gap margin; see section 7 below.
A continuous arbitrary real normalization is not enough for analyticity.

4. Delta domain, transfer, and factorial summation: yes

The stated image of the unit disk is correct. The larger Delta domain
can be justified particularly simply: choose eta>0 with
log(2+eta)/omega < g, and choose 0<eta'<pi/2. Throughout the proposed
Delta domain, |1-u|<2+eta and |arg(1-u)|<pi/2+eta'. Hence
Re t> -log(2+eta)/omega > -g and |Im t|<(pi/2+eta')/omega.
This supplies the full dented disk, not just a real approach to u=1.
Near 1, Re t tends to +infinity, so the strip growth lemma applies.
The omitted factor |t|^c is harmless: its logarithm is O(log log(1/|1-u|)),
which is still sub-polynomial in 1/|1-u|.

The O-transfer step is valid componentwise. For any fixed epsilon>0,
phi(u)=O((1-u)^(-epsilon)) in a Delta domain implies
c_k=O(k^(epsilon-1)). One may take 0<epsilon<1 and arbitrarily small;
there is no excluded polynomial-exponent issue in this use. An applicable
primary reference is Flajolet–Odlyzko, "Singularity analysis of generating
functions," SIAM J. Discrete Math. 3 (1990), 216–240, Theorem 1:
https://algo.inria.fr/flajolet/Publications/FlOd90b.pdf
The draft's Flajolet–Sedgewick VI.3 is the appropriate kind of O-transfer
citation. The conclusion uses analyticity in the entire Delta domain;
a bound only on u in (0,1) would not suffice.

For complex x=R/omega, replace the unsigned expression
sum |c_k| B(k+1,x) by sum ||c_k|| |B(k+1,x)|. On compact subsets of
Re x>0 the gamma quotient is uniformly O(k^(-Re x)). Thus, taking
epsilon<inf Re x, the terms are bounded by C*k^(-1-Re x+epsilon).
For interchange with the integral use the positive majorant
sum ||c_k|| B(k+1,Re x), or equivalently
sum ||c_k|| u^k=O((1-u)^(-epsilon)). This rigorously handles complex R.
The Stirling formula and the factor 1/omega are correct.

5. Identification: conclusion correct; one proof step needs replacement

Integration by parts and Watson's lemma work with the derivative bounds
from section 3. Specify R^beta=exp(beta*Log R), using the holomorphic Log
on Re R>0 that is real on positive R.

Uniqueness follows from the one-dimensional compound dichotomy below
Re sigma+g/2. However lines 182–183 assert more than the given leading
asymptotic: two solutions with the same leading term have difference
o(e^(Re sigma R)*R^(Re beta)), not necessarily
 o(e^(Re sigma R)*R^(Re beta-1)). The weaker estimate already puts the
difference in the same one-dimensional slow line and proves uniqueness.

The substantial missing justification is lines 187–189. The definition
of S_E only bounds individual columns by max_S Re lambda+delta. Wedge
norm submultiplicativity therefore gives m*max_S Re lambda+m*delta,
NOT Re sigma+m*delta. When slow rates differ these are different.

Repair: apply asymptotic block diagonalization for the separated slow
and fast spectral groups to A0+O(1/R). In coordinates tending to a fixed
spectral splitting, the selected block is A_S+o(1). If H(R) is its
fundamental matrix, Liouville's formula gives

 det H(R) = const * exp(integral tr(A_S+o(1)) dR)
          = exp(sigma R+o(R)) times a nonzero constant.

The coordinate maps and their exterior powers remain bounded, so the
slow wedge is O(exp((Re sigma+epsilon)R)) for every epsilon>0. It lies
in the unique compound slow line. This argument works for defective
A_S and nonreal spectra, and proves decomposability of Xi_rec.
Use a separated-block/dichotomy theorem rather than unqualified scalar
Levinson asymptotics for defective blocks. Also define S_E using a fixed
0<delta<g (or state independence of this choice); this avoids ambiguity
about the sign of delta in the current definition.

6. Interior Frobenius series: correct with explicit conventions

The matrices H,J,L and exponents K_i,-K_i-4 are correct. State explicitly
that K_i are the fixed nonnegative integer channel labels. The regular
space has dimension m for this system, with the stated resonant seed
normalization. "Regular" here permits the forced logarithms; it should
not be interpreted as holomorphic at R=0 or automatically smooth there.

Regular-singular Frobenius convergence extends to every finite radius
on a chosen logarithmic sheet, since the coefficient matrices have no
other finite singularity. Logarithmic degree is bounded in this finite
regular-singular system. Parameter-uniform majorants, with the seed
normalization fixed analytically in E, justify differentiating the series
and using it in a holomorphic matching function. State local uniformity
in R away from zero as well as E. No convergence at infinity is asserted.

"Coddington–Levinson Ch. 4, Thm 4.x" is an unfinished citation, and the
Balser and Wasow references lack sufficiently precise bibliographic data
to verify the claimed locations. Replace these by actual edition/theorem
references or include the short Fuchsian majorant argument. I have not
verified the asserted chapter/section numbers in those books; they should
not be presented as checked citations.

7. Uniformity and analyticity in energy: local repairs required

In (c), g means g(E), so choosing omega at one E does NOT authorize
local-uniform claims over all of {Re R>0} x J. Correct options:

 * Define J_omega={E in J: g(E)>log(2)/omega}, and assert local uniformity
   there; or
 * restrict to a compact energy set with positive minimum gap g0 and
   take omega>log(2)/g0.

For a simple zero E*, first fix omega>log(2)/g(E*). Continuity supplies
an energy neighborhood with a strict common margin. Then complexify E
in a sufficiently small disk. Use the Riesz spectral projector to select
the same group, and sigma=tr(A0*P_S); individual eigenvalues need not be
analytic at internal crossings. The compound eigenvalue remains simple.
Choose xi0(E) holomorphically, e.g. impose a fixed linear functional
ell(xi0)=1 where ell is nonzero at E*. This also makes beta, the formal
coefficients, and the canonical Borel germ holomorphic. Do not normalize
by a Euclidean norm, a phase chosen pointwise, or a top minor that might
vanish. Uniform Fuchsian and strip estimates on a slightly smaller complex
disk then give uniform factorial convergence there. This supplies the
complex neighborhood needed for root convergence; uniform convergence
only on real compact subsets of J does not supply it by itself.

"Any frame G_E" is appropriate for the zero condition at a fixed energy,
but not for analyticity in E: an arbitrary E-dependent rescaling may be
nonanalytic. Use an analytic frame or the canonical compound section.
Changing analytic frames multiplies the matching function by an analytic
nonvanishing factor, not necessarily an E-independent constant.

8. Abel's identity and truncated zeros

At line 40 the sign is wrong. If M=[F G;F' G'], then
(det M)'=tr(A)*det M. The invariant scalar is

 exp(-integral_{R0}^R tr A(s) ds)*det M(R)
 = exp(2 tr(U)(R-R0))*(R/R0)^(5m)*det M(R).

The same sign needs correction at line 205. At a fixed matching radius
this error does not change zeros, but it invalidates the asserted radius
independence of the displayed definition. The raw complementary-minor
pairing differs from the invariant determinant by this explicit nonzero
factor. The previously checked minor signs remain consistent.

Line 84 must say "for all sufficiently large N_F and K," not "each
truncation." Choose a small closed disk about E* containing no other
exact zeros and no zeros on its boundary. Uniform holomorphic convergence
and Rouche's theorem give exactly one zero counting multiplicity for all
N_F>=N0, K>=K0; it converges to E*. Hurwitz gives the same conclusion
with the appropriate multiplicity formulation, but Rouche makes the
two-index assertion immediate. A complex simple zero produces a real
nearby truncated zero if the normalization/truncations respect real
conjugation symmetry; state this if the intended zeros are real energies.

9. Remark 1: ratios work, but the graph hypothesis is additional

The minor-ratio description of Y is correct wherever Xi_top!=0.
However (G) does not imply xi0,top!=0 or existence of a slow solvent Y0.
For an explicit symmetric-pencil counterexample take m=2,
U=diag(2,-2), T=diag(3,3), D=0 and any allowed diagonal Lambda.
The roots are -3,-1 in channel 1 and 1,3 in channel 2. The m=2 split
has gap 2, but its slow generalized space is the two-dimensional phase
space of channel 1. Its projection to the top Phi coordinates has rank
one. Thus xi0,top=0 and no slow graph Y0 exists. This example fits the
stated matrix hypotheses (take W=3I and E=0 locally).

Add the transversality hypothesis det(projection_top|S(A0))!=0 to this
part of the remark. The compound theorem itself needs no such hypothesis.
A zero of Xi_top is a chart failure; some individual minor ratios may
have removable singularities there, so avoid labeling every denominator
zero as a pole of every entry.

The inference "positive-real asymptotic, hence finitely many poles in
Re R>=delta" is not valid from a real-ray asymptotic alone. It is,
however, repairable using estimates already available here. Integration
by parts gives

 xi(R)=xi0+h(0)/R+(1/R)*integral_0^infinity exp(-Rt)h'(t)dt.

For Re R>=delta>0, the integral is bounded uniformly by
integral exp(-delta*t)||h'(t)||dt<infinity. Hence
xi(R)=xi0+O_delta(1/|R|) uniformly throughout that closed half-plane.
Under the added nonzero-top-minor hypothesis, xi_top is bounded away
from zero there for sufficiently large |R|. The remaining region is
compactly contained in Re R>0, so it has only finitely many zeros.
This proves the desired finiteness statement with the missing hypothesis
and complex-uniform estimate supplied.

The pole obstruction for a DIRECT Y factorial series is valid. Equality
of its abscissa with a pole boundary remains unproved. Nonlinear Borel
singularities are generally governed by graph linearization eigenvalue
differences (fast-minus-slow differences, with the appropriate negative
Borel sign), and their additive combinations. Those need not equal the
full compound set sigma-sigma'. Avoid asserting that precise singularity
set without a derivation. Also, pole locations alone need not determine
a factorial-series abscissa without a separate infinity-growth argument.

10. Remark 2 and final recommended claim

The tail estimate is valid as an upper bound:
for each epsilon>0 the tail is O(K^(-Re R/omega+epsilon)), locally
uniformly with the corresponding parameter margins. It gives no lower
bound and does not establish an EXACT abscissa of zero. Replace
"the theorem's abscissa is 0" by "the series converges absolutely for
Re R>0 (its abscissa is at most 0)." Special solutions can even make
the series terminate. For example m=1, U=0, T=-1, D=5, Lambda=0 has
slow solution Phi=exp(-R), beta=0, and h identically zero at that energy.
Its compound correction series terminates despite a positive gap.

With the repairs above, the defensible result is:

For fixed finite-channel matrices and fixed integer channel labels, near
each energy satisfying the separated m/m gap, choose omega with a strict
local common gap margin and fix analytic seed/exterior normalizations.
The regular Fock series and the canonical linear-compound factorial
series converge locally uniformly (also in complex energy near that
point), and their complementary-minor pairing defines the exact finite
model matching condition at every positive matching radius. Near a simple
zero, all sufficiently large two-index truncations have one nearby zero
converging to it. This establishes the two-series determinant construction
for the linear Plucker representation. It does not establish convergence
of the directly computed nonlinear Y factorial expansion.
