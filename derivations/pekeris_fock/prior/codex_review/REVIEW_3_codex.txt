Review of REQUEST_3_plucker.txt — 2026-10-19

Verdict

(ii) is correct as a representation of the fixed finite-channel boundary
problem, PROVIDED the exterior factorial series converges at the chosen
matching radius and the root is isolated. Computing E and the regular
origin amplitudes together in sufficient precision removes the purported
fundamental origin-data barrier. This does not remove numerical conditioning,
angular-model error, or the need to establish exterior convergence. The
current matching code is not an arbitrary-precision implementation of this
proposal.

(i) is a plausible explanation of the observed slow Plucker convergence,
not a demonstrated convergence theorem. In particular, the data establish
neither a positive Plucker abscissa nor a zero one. Zero is the natural
candidate for this homogeneous regular-singular problem. There is a much
more concrete route to proving it than fitting angles: derive the linear
Borel equation and bound its growth at infinity. The apparent positive
abscissae are entirely compatible with large powers of log K.

For d=6 at R*=10, a useful starting prescription for approximately 16-digit
work is a genuinely BigFloat interior at 256 bits and Fock order 200,
followed by precision/order checks of the determinant, root, and null vector.
Order 180 may suffice; order 160 is marginal for an absolute 1e-12 full-state
target. These are numerical starting settings, not certified error bounds
or a claim that 256 bits suffice for every exterior coefficient conversion.

I read the plucker sources, logs, CSVs and figure, the earlier reviews, and
the underlying interior recurrence. I ran audit3.jl locally (audit3.log,
512 bits, and audit3_256.log, 256 bits). No files in ../exterior_factorial
were modified. The checks use the same supplied model matrices; they do
not independently validate the angular discretization or physical branch.

1. Code audit: the main algebra is correct

The first-order blocks have the right signs. The additive compound is
constructed by differentiating each wedge factor, which is the additive,
not multiplicative, compound. Lexicographic ordering is used consistently.
The graph-recovery sign (-1)^(m-j) and complementary-minor pairing sign
(-1)^(sum(S)+m(m+1)/2) are correct.

With B0=C0-sigma I and B1=C1-beta I, the normalized equation is
 xi' = (B0+B1/R+C2/R^2) xi.
Its recurrence is
 B0 xi_n = ((beta-n+1)I-C1) xi_{n-1} - C2 xi_{n-2}.
The solvability condition gives beta=w'C1 xi0/(w'xi0). Finalizing xi_{n-1}
by adding alpha xi0 gives exactly the implemented denominator (n-1)w'xi0.
The loop runs through N+1, so all RETURNED coefficients through xi_N are
finalized. This fixes the endpoint issue previously found in per_exponent.
The rank-one regularization Ms+xi0*w' is valid without requiring w'xi0=1.

Independent local checks at 512 bits gave:
 beta=0.9108379336484201;
 beta-tr(Y1) approximately 6.0e-154;
 recurrence residual/norm(xi_n) at n=1,2,20,60 at most 1.3e-153;
 random-frame compound derivative residual about 1.6e-58 (step 1e-60);
 determinant/minor pairing residual about 1.5e-154.
The identity beta=tr(Y1) is a useful independent normalization check: the
scalar top-block determinant of a frame grows with logarithmic derivative
tr(Y), so its power at infinity is tr(Y1). There is no beta sign error.
The Stirling conversion and Pochhammer indexing are also consistent.

The left inverse iteration has a robustness limitation, not an observed
failure: it uses random initialization and a fixed Float64 literal 1e-200
shift. It is not precision-scaled, and accuracy of its result depends on
the eigenspace conditioning. Check the left residual and w'xi0 explicitly;
use a bordered solve or adaptive shift for a reusable implementation.
The tests above found no resulting defect at these parameters.

2. The angle is correct, but it cannot measure scalar convergence

proj_dist is the Hermitian projective sine distance. The residual-vector
formula is preferable to sqrt(1-|dot|^2) at small angles. It correctly
ignores any nonzero scalar, including sign/phase, and is appropriate for
comparing subspaces in homogeneous coordinates.

That invariance is exactly why it is NOT a convergence test for xi with its
specified infinity normalization. A sequence c_K xi_ref with c_K divergent
would have zero angle. A large scalar truncation error parallel to xi_ref
is invisible. Moreover, truncated compound sums need not be decomposable;
one should monitor Plucker-relation residuals before interpreting a finite
sum as an actual frame. Graph ratios alone do not test decomposability.

Record scalar-normalized vector errors against a consistently normalized
linear-compound reference, raw component/tail increments, norm growth, and
Plucker relations as well as angles. For matching, also record the pairing
error and its derivative with respect to E.

The summary's comparison of the two methods mixes a bounded angle and an
unbounded matrix norm. It is not a common error metric or timing benchmark.
At omega=.224, K=800, R=.5, the supplied CSV/log give angle .21, Y recovered
FROM PLUCKER error about 8.1, and DIRECT Y-chart error 8.4e4. Thus the claim
that Plucker is worse at every R<=3 is false if it means boundary-map error.
At R=1 the analogous errors are .17 versus .27. At R=1.5,2,3 the direct
Y-chart is substantially more accurate. Neither representation gives high
accuracy at .5 at this tested order, but there is no universal dominance.
"Nothing converges usefully below 1.5" needs an accuracy target and a term
budget; it is not a mathematical conclusion.

3. What the origin singularity actually says

The displayed A2/R^2 makes the first-order system look irregular at zero,
but use Z=(Phi,R Phi') instead. Then
 Z' = [H/R + J + R L] Z,
 H = [0 I; Lambda -4I],
 J = [0 0; -D -2U],   L = [0 0; -T 0].
This is regular singular (Poincare rank zero). Its indicial roots are
 K_i and -K_i-4. Exterior powers remain regular singular in these variables.
Returning to (Phi,Phi') multiplies a minor with r derivative rows by R^-r.
The additional R^-beta gauge changes powers, and exp(-sigma R) is analytic
and nonzero at zero. One therefore expects powers and logarithms, not a
new finite positive-R singularity of the homogeneous vector.

For d=2, K=[0,2,4,4]. A generic most singular all-irregular wedge, in the
all-derivative minor, has exponent -sum(K)-5m=-30. After the power gauge,
xi can therefore contain R^-q with q=30+beta=30.9108379. This is an upper
candidate singular power, not proof its connection coefficient is nonzero
in the selected exterior solution. At an exact matched energy the exterior
space intersects the regular interior space, so the all-irregular
connection determinant vanishes; leading powers can disappear. Individual
components/projections may also cancel. Logarithmic resonances add further
factors. There is ample room for substantial finite-order transients.

Here is the relevant asymptotic calculation. If xi contains c R^-q, its
Laplace Borel density behaves like c t^(q-1)/Gamma(q). Under
 t=-log(1-u)/omega
this is a power of log(1/(1-u)), not an algebraic pole (1-u)^-p. For q>1,
the leading Taylor coefficients of this endpoint behavior are proportional
to (log k)^(q-2)/k. The factorial tail is consequently of the form
 K^(-R/omega) (log K)^(q-2)
up to constants, lower log powers, and any extra log-log factors inherited
from origin logarithms. The corresponding local fitted abscissa is
 p_eff approximately omega (q-2)/log K,
even though its limit is zero. Its drift with omega is expected. The
candidate generic log power here is about 28.91; cancellation and
preasymptotic behavior can readily change an effective 19–24 fit.
This calculation concerns scalar components/tails in their asymptotic
regime. Fitting projective errors at angle .1–.5 is not a measurement of q.

At R=3 the Y-chart errors are at the Float64 reference floor; at R=5 both
methods hit it. Fitting a slope to that floor gives p_eff=R and has no
convergence meaning. The figure's rising Y abscissa at R=3 is this artifact.

4. A concrete route to the Plucker abscissa

Let xi=xi0+Laplace[h], with h(t)=sum xi_{n+1} t^n/n!. Borel-transform the
normalized equation after multiplying by R^2. Away from t=0, it gives
 (t I+B0) h'' + (2I+B1) h' + C2 h = 0.
Its finite candidate singularities are t=sigma-lambda(C0), including zero;
the selected formal branch is analytic at zero. For the tested d=2 spectrum,
the nonzero candidates are negative real, beginning at -3.556. This linear
equation is more informative than a finite scalar Pade plot: it identifies
all possible finite singular locations of this Borel system.

The logarithmic map sends |u|<1 into Re(t)>-log(2)/omega. At omega=.224 the
left boundary is about -3.094, strictly to the right of the nearest
candidate. Thus no nonzero finite Borel singularity enters the disk. The
remaining issue is growth as u approaches 1, i.e. t tends to infinity in a
horizontal strip. A polynomial/logarithmic bound on h there, as suggested
by the regular-singular origin after the shear, gives a convergent factorial
series for every real R>0. One can then prove the zero abscissa (provided
an actual origin singularity remains) and logarithmic tail estimates.

This is my preferred theorem candidate and proof route. I have not supplied
the required uniform Borel-growth proof or a certified bound in this
review. Regularity of xi away from zero, Pade poles, and finite K data
alone cannot substitute for it. A positive factorial abscissa is not
logically ruled out just by pole-free finite-R continuation: factorial
convergence also depends on transformed-Borel analyticity/growth. For THIS
linear regular-singular model, zero has a strong structural explanation;
there is no numerical evidence here requiring a genuine positive abscissa.

Pade details: the [60/60] nearest poles match the negative-gap picture.
However [40/40] in the supplied log also produces +.581 and -2.989 poles.
They are inconsistent with the exact candidate set and illustrate spurious
Pade roots. The code gives no final root residuals, convergence report,
or numerator-zero/Froissart check. Do not promote “only negative poles at
[60/60]” to a global Borel analyticity or growth certificate. Negative-axis
finite Borel singularities themselves do not determine the Laplace
abscissa; growth at positive Borel infinity is crucial.

5. Y-chart abscissa: rightmost pole is an obstruction, not yet an equality

A genuine pole p of Y forces the abscissa of a single factorial
representation on a right half-plane to be at least Re(p). Otherwise the
convergent representation would be analytic through that pole. The known
d=2 pair with Re(p)=.637277 is therefore a genuine lower obstruction for
this chart. The scalar factorial expansion of 1/(R-p) makes its contribution
explicit: the tail is proportional to K^((p-R)/omega).

Equality with the supremum of all pole real parts is a reasonable theorem
candidate only with additional assumptions: control ALL poles, the origin
boundary behavior, growth needed for Laplace inversion, and analyticity
and boundary bounds of the transformed Borel function for the chosen
omega. Under suitable such assumptions the expected threshold is the
maximum of the origin boundary 0 and the rightmost pole real part.
The nearest pole found by a bounded scan is not necessarily the rightmost.
Complex pole pairs also cause log-K oscillations and large constants;
finite-range log-error slopes need not equal their envelope exponent.
No supplied data prove this equality, particularly for d=6.

6. Two convergent series can define the finite-model eigenvalue

Let F(E,R) be the regular interior frame and G(E,R) the selected exterior
frame. For m channels the condition is
 det [ F(E,R*)  G(E,R*) ; F'(E,R*)  G'(E,R*) ] = 0.
Equivalently, if the chosen exterior top block is invertible, use
 det(F'-Y F)=0. This avoids forming F^-1. The exterior Plucker pairing is
the homogeneous version. Recover the interior amplitudes from the null
vector after solving for E, with a fixed amplitude normalization.

The determinant vanishing is independent of matching radius for exact
solutions, by invertible linear evolution and Abel's identity. Series that
converge locally uniformly in E near an isolated simple root can therefore
be used to compute it. The Fock representation is generalized Frobenius,
with logarithms: calling the solution “entire” is literally incorrect at
zero. For this finite ODE with no other finite coefficient singularities,
the regular logarithmic expansion has no finite positive-R convergence
barrier on the chosen logarithmic sheet. This is sufficient for matching.

The distinction from propagating uncertain E and a is decisive. If those
are supplied with fixed error, outward continuation can magnify it beyond
a requested tolerance. If they are unknowns solved consistently in higher
precision, the same amplification is a condition number to budget for,
not an impossibility. Additional losses from nonnormality, powers of R,
frame conditioning, near-multiple roots, and a poorly chosen amplitude
normalization must also be included. There is no universal exact loss
formula involving just one gap.

R*=3 for d=2 and R*=8–10 for d=6 are useful empirically supported working
radii for the tested term counts. They are not proven convergence
boundaries. Nor does “two-series determinant” constitute a closed form
in a stronger classical special-function or finite-expression sense.
It is a legitimate implicit convergent-series characterization when its
convergence hypotheses hold. It does not solve the full untruncated
helium problem or establish a speed advantage over panels.

7. d=6 at R*=10: measured Fock order and precision budget

I streamed the same Fock recurrence in BigFloat through N=280, retaining
two seed columns rather than storing an N-squared coefficient tensor. One
seed was the supplied approximate cuspsolve amplitude; the other was a
normalized random vector. The test includes values and first derivatives,
and the original logarithmic terms. At 512 bits, combined (Phi,Phi')
errors against N=280 were:

 order N     supplied approximate amplitude     generic normalized seed
    120                  5.85                       1.09e7
    140                  1.48e-8                    2.35e-2
    160                  2.25e-18                   3.14e-12
    180                  2.89e-29                   3.65e-23
    200                  4.15e-41                   4.80e-35
    220                  8.25e-54                   8.86e-48

The 256-bit rerun reproduced the displayed order comparisons through N=220
to the printed three-digit accuracy. At larger orders some differences
reach the arithmetic floor; a zero difference there is not a tail bound.
This compares order behavior, not high-precision roots or all final digits
across the two precisions.

These are truncation comparisons at the supplied Float64 energy for two
seeds, not uniform operator bounds over all interior seeds or a high-
precision eigenvalue calculation. The approximate matched seed is NOT an
accurate slow solution at R=10: its final norm is about 2.6e5 because the
input E/a errors have already generated fast contamination. Its small
truncation errors do not validate that approximate physical solution.

For the generic seed, sum of norms of all evaluated Fock terms was about
1.08e24; for the approximate matched seed it was 3.62e18. This illustrates
the arithmetic scale to budget for. The d=6 A0 spectra in this test are
 slow: -.28096 to .57473; fast: 3.37756 to 4.93239.
A conservative spectral separation over R=10 is about 5.213*10/ln(10)
=22.6 decimal digits, or 75 bits. To retain 16 digits of a selected slow
state, start with roughly 40 significant digits PLUS guard digits for
powers, nonnormality, matrix conditioning, and root extraction. At 256
bits there are about 77 decimal digits; 192 bits may work, while 128 bits
is marginal. For roughly 30-digit work, start with 384 bits and N=220,
then verify. The order table is evidence for these choices, not a proof
of a minimal required order or precision.

Use the entire interior matrix and check its matching singular values,
the energy derivative, and the amplitude vector under N and precision
increases. Arbitrary precision applied after a Float64 recurrence does
not help. The exterior Stirling transformation has its OWN cancellation
budget (the prior d=6 experiments used 768–1024 bits), and must also retain
that precision through the determinant. A 256-bit interior recommendation
is not a recommendation to reduce exterior working precision to 256 bits.

8. What the current matching experiment proves

The results at rm=1,2,3 credibly show decreasing error against the panel
implementation of the same finite model: approximately 4.5e-5, 1.1e-10,
and indistinguishable Float64 energies respectively. They are a useful
cross-check of the representation, not a verified arbitrary-precision root.

interior_wedge calls cuspcoeff and evalseries in Float64 and only THEN
converts [F;D] to BigFloat. E and the secant iterates are Float64;
f_plucker and f_panel explicitly return Float64. The rm=3 panel pairing
residual is 6.3e-14 even though the energy step stops below 1e-15. The
secant routine checks step size, not residual/slope-based energy error.
Thus identical 15-decimal energies do not establish a 1e-15 error bound.
At R=3 the angle 5.9e-14 and the recovered-Y error 4.1e-15 differ because
projective/frame conditioning and direction of error matter. State-specific
pairing error may be smaller still.

For a convincing implementation of (ii), make cuspcoeff, evalseries, E,
the scalar matching function and root finder genuinely generic in precision;
retain exterior precision; recover a with a high-precision null solve; and
vary Fock order, factorial order, precision and matching radius. Use a
residual divided by a checked nonzero energy derivative to estimate root
error. Keep this numerical error separate from the fixed Float64 angular
matrix/model error. None of these requirements calls for modifying the
mathematical two-series construction.
