## What the classical comparison actually says

DEDUCTION from the displayed scalar ratios: if log(a_(n+1)/a_n)=+/-c/sqrt(n)+O(1/n), summing gives log a_n=+/-2c sqrt(n)+O(log n), with the usual nonzero-branch qualifications. The separation is stretched exponential in sqrt(n), not exp(+/-k*n). Both leading roots can be 1 and scalar ratio-minimality can still hold. On Stokes/exceptional parameter sets where the real part of c vanishes the inference of a decaying ratio is unavailable; subleading terms and boundary conditions require separate analysis. The generic root-1,root-1 radial structure suffices to reject the proposed blanket explanation by distinct Poincaré–Perron roots.

The three-term scalar problem has a two-dimensional formal solution space, a meaningful comparison of two scalar sequences, and a continued fraction for their ratio. The helium shell blocks grow like N², outer blocks can be rectangular, and no scalar quotient or two-dimensional solution-space argument transfers automatically. Our backward procedure is precisely the physical weak-pencil finite-section solve, with bounded physical head, a dense conforming domain, vanishing omitted source at E0 and form-norm convergence. Its Schur complement can be computed with fill-in. This is a spectral definition of the selected helium solution, even without a proved ratio classification.

## What Fock nonanalyticity does and does not prove

PROVED implication for an ordinary fixed-scale polynomial Laguerre reconstruction: a uniform geometric coefficient bound |A_lmn|<=C*rho^(l+m+n), rho<1 (or a geometric shell l2 bound, which implies it), would make the reconstructed F entire in the three complex perimetric variables. Indeed

    |L_n(z)| <= sum_k (n*|z|)^k/(k!)² <= exp(2sqrt(n*|z|)).

On every compact complex set this subexponential growth, the polynomial number of shell indices, and rho^N give normal convergence, also for derivatives by holomorphy. Thus wF would have an analytic radial expansion on each generic fixed angular ray. A nonzero R² log R Fock term rules this out. Conditional only on that actual nonzero local term and equality of the series to the physical function, geometric decay of the full plain coefficient array is impossible. This does not establish an exact algebraic decay exponent, coefficientwise lower bounds, monotonicity, or a theorem for enriched coordinates. Faster-than-algebraic but slower-than-geometric rates are not excluded merely by failure of analyticity.

It also does not exclude geometric separation from a *growing* formal solution. A simple exact counterexample has h_n=1/(n+1), d_n=2^n, n>=0. Both solve, for n>=1,

    u_(n+1) - (3n²+11n+8)/(n+2)²*u_n
      + (2n²+6n)/(n+2)²*u_(n-1)=0.

The limiting characteristic roots are 1 and 2. h_n is algebraic, yet h_n/d_n tends to zero geometrically times a power. This is separation with a center branch, not a uniform stable/unstable exponential dichotomy with h in an exponentially decaying stable bundle. Fock logs alone cannot rule it out.

A narrower conditional exclusion is valid: if an asserted shell realization has a physical stable bundle decaying as C*exp(-k*(N-M)) in a state norm uniformly equivalent to the raw coefficient shell norm, then a nonzero physical stable state would have geometric raw coefficients, contradicting the preceding analytic obstruction. Uniform norm equivalence, reconstruction and absence of a center branch are essential hypotheses. Singular/energy-dependent changes of coordinates, growing block sizes and admissibility can invalidate them. This conditional observation is not a completed asymptotic analysis of the helium transfer problem.

NUMERICAL evidence: Claude's finite raw-tail experiments show undamped responses on the tested shells and algebraic physical shell decay. They are evidence against a useful geometric backward contraction in that representation and range. They do not prove nonexistence of every asymptotic dichotomy or exclude slower ratio-minimality. Therefore the broad statement 'no exponential dichotomy for helium-type recurrences' remains OPEN, not a theorem established by those plots.

## Would another basis restore separation?

PROVED general observation: bounded invertible changes of shell-state coordinates with uniform bounds preserve a uniform exponential dichotomy and its failure (transport the invariant projections and estimates). Unbounded rescalings need not: multiplying coordinates by exp(kN) changes absolute growth rates. Merely renormalizing columns can create apparent decay without improving physical error or relative minimality; common scalar rescaling leaves scalar ratios of independent solutions unchanged.

Changing fixed eps to another positive fixed eps in an ordinary polynomial Laguerre basis retains the Fock analyticity obstruction above. An energy-dependent scale chosen equal to a positive constant at E0 has the same obstruction at that energy. It may improve finite convergence or other asymptotic channels; it does not prove a uniform dichotomy. A true Coulomb Sturmian basis, hyperspherical/Fock-adapted functions, an infinite log tower, or a different shell grouping changes the approximation and recurrence problem more substantially. Explicit singular functions can remove the particular analyticity obstruction from their remaining coefficients. Finite log/atom enrichment does not by itself prove analytic remainders at all orders or exponential rates.

CONJECTURAL research possibilities: a Fock-adapted basis could produce stronger separation in selected channels; a suitable weighted operator/Weyl-space analysis could classify an admissible subspace without any scalar minimal ratio. Neither is proved here. A restoration claim needs an explicit recurrence/transfer realization, norm, reconstruction theorem, invariant splitting and uniform constants, with endpoint and energy dependence controlled. Choosing an exact eigenbasis would trivially sparsify the physical eigenvector but is not a computable finite-recurrence construction.

For the special-function framing the proved result is already concrete: exact operator/weak pencil plus normalization plus a convergent zero-tail limit selects the ground state, and near-ground omitted-row zeros select its energy. The classical scalar continued-fraction analogy is structural, not an identification of characteristic roots, universal geometric convergence, or a finished Pincherle theorem for every formal helium solution.
