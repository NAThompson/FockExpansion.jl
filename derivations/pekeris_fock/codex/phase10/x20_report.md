## 2. Torsion, the rational quotient, and sections

Let k=a1 P2-a2 P1. Exact algebra gives

    B²-AC=-w² k²,
    X³+2BX²+ACX = X(X+B+iwk)(X+B-iwk).

Thus there are **three** nonzero geometric 2-torsion sections, not just
(X,Y)=(0,0). The other two may require adjoining w and i to the target field.
Geometric torsion is exactly (Z/2)². Indeed, torsion injects into the product
of fibre component groups, so only 2-power torsion is possible here. The
standard halving test says a root section can be halved only if its differences
from the other two cubic roots are squares in C(q). For e2, k=1-q⁴ has simple
zeros, and the other root differences have simple zeros as well. For ±e1,
k=±2q² is a square up to a constant, but each remaining root quartic has simple
zeros generically. No nonzero 2-torsion section is divisible by 2, excluding
order 4 and hence every higher order.

The descent z=q² is a rational elliptic surface with six I2 fibres, so its
geometric MW rank is 8-6=2. Quadratic base change preserves independent
sections. For the original q fibration, Shioda–Tate therefore gives rho≥18
in the endpoint case and initially rho≥16 for e2. The exact formula and the
component-group torsion injection used here are [Schütt–Shioda, Cor. 6.13 and
Cor. 7.5](https://arxiv.org/html/0907.0298#S6.SS11).

### Three independent transverse sections

For a=e2 put A=1-v², c=1-u², ζ8=exp(πi/4). The following are actual sections:

    X0=-2Aq²,
    Y0=A sqrt(-2c) q(q⁴-1),

    X+=A[i(q⁴-1)-2q²],
    Y+=A(u+iv)ζ8^(-1)(q⁴-1)(q²+i),

    X-=A[-i(q⁴-1)-2q²],
    Y-=A(u-iv)ζ8(q⁴-1)(q²-i).

**VERIFIED:** all three polynomial substitutions vanish identically; their
nonzero Y coordinates generically exclude 2-torsion. Since all I2 component
groups have exponent 2, the torsion bound above excludes any other torsion.
Their independence has a useful exact explanation. Set

    r=q²-q^(-2), X=q² z, Y=q³ h.

The equation descends to the rational elliptic surface

    h²=z[z²+2(-uvr+2A)z+A(cr²-4uvr+4A)]             (E/r).

The field C(q)/C(r) is biquadratic, generated by

    sqrt(r+2i)=q+i/q, sqrt(r-2i)=q-i/q.

The first section descends to E/r. The other two have the distinct characters
of sqrt(r+2i) and sqrt(r-2i). On E/r their z coordinates are -2A,
iA(r+2i), -iA(r-2i), respectively. In the free MW group the three nonzero
character vectors are independent. This proves rho≥14+3=17 and explains why
q² descent alone missed a section. It does not presume an exact MW basis or
saturation index; neither is needed for the free rank once rho is known.

## 3. Transverse Picard number: an arithmetic certificate

**PROVED with CITED ordinary K3 arithmetic inputs; VERIFIED exact counts.**
At (u,v)=(1/3,1/4) all twelve singular fibres remain distinct over
characteristic zero and at p=11,13,19. Count over F_(p^n), n=1,2,3.
For Q=p^n, let S_Q be the sum of the quadratic character of
X³+2B(q)X²+AC(q)X over all X and all q in P1(F_Q), including the correctly
rescaled smooth infinity fibre. Let R_Q count the rational roots of C and
1-q⁴. Replacing each singular nodal cubic by its resolved I2 fibre adds Q,
including the nonsplit case. Consequently

    #S(F_Q)=(Q+1)²+S_Q+Q R_Q,
    Tr(Frob_Q|H²)=2Q+S_Q+Q R_Q.

The fibre/zero-section/component lattice contributes 2Q+Q R_Q. The free
section characters are also explicit. If ε=(-2(1-u²)/p), their trace is

    m_n=ε^n+χ8(p^n),
    χ8(1)=2, χ8(5)=-2, χ8(3)=χ8(7)=0.

This follows directly by Galois action on sqrt(-2c), i and ζ8 in the three
section formulas. Thus the complement of the known 17 divisors has traces
S_Q-Q m_n and dimension five. Newton identities determine its first three
coefficients. Orthogonal Poincaré reciprocity determines the remaining two
and its sign uniquely; no uncounted fourth or fifth trace is guessed.

Here P5(T)=det(1-Frob_p T) on that complement:

| p | S_p, S_(p²), S_(p³) | P5(T) |
|---:|---|---|
| 11 | -6, 168, -6486 | (1+11T)(1-16T+286T²-1936T³+14641T⁴) |
| 13 | -16, 672, -5968 | (1-13T)(1+16T+130T²+2704T³+28561T⁴) |
| 19 | -14, 1488, 6946 | (1+19T)(1-24T+266T²-8664T³+130321T⁴) |

All three remaining normalized quartics have no root of unity. This is checked
exactly against all cyclotomic polynomials of degree ≤4. Therefore each
reduction has geometric Picard upper bound 18. All known divisor characters
are fixed over F_(p⁴): C is the product of two irreducible quartics, the other
four components and all three free-section characters split in degree ≤4,
and the extra eigenvalue ±p also becomes +p⁴.

The reductions are ordinary: their H² trace is nonzero modulo p. The
ordinary K3 Tate/Artin–Tate theorem determines the NS discriminant squareclass.
If R_p is the quartic factor above and g_p(z)=z⁴ R_p(1/(pz)), the needed
Artin–Tate limit over F_(p⁴) is det(1-(Frob_p/p)⁴) on this four-dimensional
remaining part, i.e. Res(g_p(z),1-z⁴). Its values are

| p | limit | disc NS modulo rational squares |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 11 | 20480/14641 | -5 |
| 13 | 69632/28561 | -17 |
| 19 | 230400/130321 | -1 |

Here disc NS ≡ -p⁴·limit; the factor p⁴ is a square and the elliptic-surface
Brauer-group order is a square. If the characteristic-zero Picard number
were 18, its specializations would have finite index in both rank-18 NS
lattices and hence the same discriminant squareclass. They do not. Therefore
rho≤17. The three sections give rho≥17, proving **rho=17 at this rational
target and at the geometric generic target**, MW rank=3 and rank T=22-17=5.
The specialization/discriminant argument and the ordinary arithmetic input
are [van Luijk, §2, especially the discussion of Artin–Tate on p. 6](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~luijkrmvan/ps/picone.pdf).

## 4. Endpoint surface: explicit elliptic product

For a=e1, A=1-u² and B=A(q⁴-1)-2uvq². Quotient by the 2-torsion point
(X,Y)=(0,0). The degree-two map is

    X'=X+2B+AC/X, Y'=Y(1-AC/X²),
    Y'²=X'³-4BX'²+4(B²-AC)X'
       =X'³+[-4Aq⁴+8uvq²+4A]X'²-16w²q⁴X'.

It preserves dq∧dX/Y. Translation by this torsion point is symplectic;
its resolved quotient is again K3. Here is an exact identification of that
quotient, rather than an inference from its 2I8+8I1 fibres.
Put H=√A, a=v/H, b=iu/H and

    λ1=(a+1)/(a-1), λ2=(b+1)/(b-1),
    g=A(b-1)², k²=i(a-1)/(b-1).

Set t=kq, X'=g x, Y'=g^(3/2)y. The quotient becomes

    y²=x³+[(λ1-1)²t⁴-2(λ1+1)(λ2+1)t²+(λ2-1)²]x²
           +16λ1λ2t⁴x.

All four coefficient equalities and the isogeny identity are checked exactly
in x20_verify.py. This is the J1 fibration of the Kummer surface of the two
Legendre elliptic curves E_i: η_i²=z_i(z_i-1)(z_i-λ_i), as constructed in
[Kuwata–Shioda, §2.1 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0609473#S2.SS1).
They are generically nonisogenous, with independent moduli, so this Kummer
surface has rho=18. A generically finite map between these K3s identifies their
rational transcendental Hodge structures up to scaling of the pairing: pullback
is injective, and its image containing H²,⁰ is the entire simple transcendental
Hodge structure. Thus S_e1 also has rho=18; Shioda–Tate gives MW rank=2.
The case a=-e1 is obtained by (X,Y)↦(-X,iY), geometrically.

The elliptic curves can be written more simply, up to algebraic isomorphism:

    E1: η²=(1-u)t⁴-2vt²+(1+u) = E_old(x),
    E2: η²=z(z²+2uz+1).

Their exact j-invariants are

    j1=64(v²+3A)³/[A(v²-A)²],
    j2=64(3-4u²)³/A.

So one factor is exactly the old target panel curve; the second depends only
on u. Varying j in the original q fibration did not exclude this reduction.

**Classification precision:** this proves *product-period type by an explicit
Nikulin 2-isogeny to a product Kummer*. It does not identify the integral
transcendental lattice of S_e1 or prove that this particular isogeny satisfies
the stricter integral definition of a Shioda–Inose structure. Nor is an
isomorphism of S_e1 itself to the product Kummer asserted. Those finer lattice
questions are **OPEN here** and are unnecessary for the period-class decision.
For e2, rank T=5 excludes product-of-two-elliptic-period type. It does not by
itself exclude a Kummer related to a *simple* abelian surface; that is a different
classification question and no such identification is claimed.

## 5. Monodromy, period order and the special endpoint line

**PROVED using CITED Hodge-theoretic inputs:** the transverse transcendental
variation has connected algebraic monodromy SO(T), real signature (2,3),
complex standard representation SO5. The argument is as follows.

1. The generic rank-five transcendental Hodge structure of a projective K3 is
   simple. Its Hodge endomorphism field K is totally real or CM. Its degree
   divides 5, so CM is impossible. Degree 5 would give dim_K T=1, impossible:
   the (2,0) and (0,2) lines would lie in the same one-dimensional complex
   eigenspace for the distinguished real embedding. Thus K=Q. Zarhin's Hodge
   group theorem gives Hdg(T)=SO(T). The statements used are recorded, with
   an elementary endomorphism-field argument, in [Huybrechts, Ch. 3, Thm. 3.7,
   Thm. 3.9 and Remark 3.14](https://www.math.uni-bonn.de/people/huybrech/K3Global.pdf).
2. The family is nonisotrivial. Mark the elliptic fibre/section classes and the
   twelve I2 component classes after a finite cover. The four cross fibres
   q⁴=1 fix the base coordinate up to a finite group. The normalized C
   coefficients vary with

       f=uv/(1-u²), h=(1+u²-2v²)/(1-u²),
       det ∂(f,h)/∂(u,v)=-4(u²+v²)/(1-u²)³ ≠ 0.

   These are therefore genuinely varying marked elliptic K3s. Along v=1/3
   they are still nonconstant, and the arithmetic certificate ensures that
   their very general Picard number is 17.
3. The connected algebraic monodromy group is normal in the derived generic
   Mumford–Tate group. This is [André, §5, Thm. 1](https://www.numdam.org/article/CM_1992__82_1_1_0.pdf),
   applied to the smooth projective K3 variation on its regular algebraic base.
   SO5 is simple. Trivial connected monodromy would be finite monodromy; after
   finite cover the fixed-part theorem would make this entire variation
   constant, contradicting nonisotriviality and local Torelli. Hence it is SO5.

This proves irreducibility even after finite algebraic base change. Nonzero
closed holomorphic periods therefore have scalar minimal order 5 on the
certified transverse line; otherwise their monodromy orbits would span fewer
than five solutions. In two parameters the pure connection has rank 5.
No tensor product of two rank-two elliptic systems, symmetric square (rank 3),
or symmetric fourth power of a rank-two elliptic system has this connected
monodromy in this representation. This is a genuine new *auxiliary pure*
period class, not a proof about the physical solution.

For the endpoint family the two varying elliptic factors give connected
monodromy (SL2×SL2)/{±(1,1)} on their tensor product, equivalently SO(2,2)
over the real numbers and SO4 over C. The two independent j moduli give this
product on the two-parameter family. A generic line varying both factors has
order 4; an isogeny locus can reduce to a symmetric-square rank-three part.

On u=u0 fixed, E2 is constant. The period functions reduce to two varying
elliptic periods with constant multipliers. More precisely the holomorphic
form pulls back with factor 1/(k√g), proportional to (v-H)^(-1/2), H²=1-u0².
A Gauss period F(λ1) satisfies

    λ1(1-λ1) F''+(1-2λ1)F'-F/4=0.

The exact substitution Π=(v-H)^(-1/2)F((v+H)/(v-H)) gives

    [(v²-H²)D_v²+2vD_v+1/4] Π=0.

For u0=1/5 this is [(v²-24/25)D_v²+2vD_v+1/4]Π=0. Local exponents are
(0,0) at v=±H and (1/2,1/2) at infinity; logarithmic companions give the
usual elliptic unipotent monodromies, with a minus sign at infinity.
The full rank-four local system on this line consists, over C, of two copies
of the varying rank-two elliptic system; its scalar holomorphic periods need
only order 2. This distinction prevents a misleading comparison with a generic
rank-four cyclic connection.

**OPEN / delegated computation:** rational PF coefficients for the transverse
line, its individual local monodromy matrices, apparent singularities and
exponent table have not been computed here. An exact telescoper for a chosen
rational presentation can have additional factors; the irreducible *closed
holomorphic transcendental factor* is the order-five object specified above.

## 6. Which object occurs in ψ40?

**PROVED algebraic-measure statement:** in the source-independent two-Coulomb
reduction the algebraic two-dimensional measure is the holomorphic two-form.
Indeed the external density is 1/√D; the beta measure is dt/√(t(1-t)); the
angular radical is D/√Q, with Q as in x19. Together they are

    √D dq dt/√(t(1-t)Q) = D dq dt/Y0,
    Y0²=D t(1-t) Q.

With s=t/(1-t), X=ADs, Y=A(1+s)²Y0, the equality is exactly

    D dq∧dt/Y0 = dq∧dX/Y.

This verifies why S_a and its holomorphic form are relevant. It does **not**
mean the physical function is a closed period of this form. The zonal inverse
still supplies elementary/logarithmic factors and polynomial numerators; the
q,t domain has boundaries. Most importantly, the physical panel density
contains atan(q/h(y))² with y the source point. It is not a target-only
multiplier. Its exact rational representation is

    atan(q/h(y))²=(1-b²) ∫_0^q dr ∫_0^q ds /
        [(1+b+(1-b)r²)(1+b+(1-b)s²)],
    h²=(1+b)/(1-b), b=b(y).

Those two additional parameters must remain inside the angular inverse. Thus
the full contribution is a relative/iterated period in a larger algebraic
family, with additional divisors and boundaries. The bare S_a holomorphic
period can be a graded constituent or can cancel; geometry alone does not
choose between these outcomes. Annihilating closed ω periods gives a
homogeneous PF equation; integrating over the physical open chain generally
has boundary terms, hence an inhomogeneous equation or a larger extension.

**OPEN:** a nonzero projection onto T(S_e2) after all physical weights/sums.
The even electron projection can select or cancel particular components.
Keeping a generic symbolic Z distinguishes charge-sector cancellation from
panel cancellation; Z=2 then needs its own nonvanishing check. Pure harmonic
subtractions are algebraic, but must be included when defining the source.
The bounded old-module fits in x19 are not a proof of nonmembership and
supply no answer to this period-pairing question.

A decisive cheap target for Claude's computation is a *physical discontinuity*
or a certified relative Gauss–Manin projection, rather than a larger real fit:
apply the rank-five quotient/projection to each actual weighted panel source,
assemble its electron-even, charge-correct coefficient, and exhibit one
nonzero component. Equivalently, continue the actual full chain around a
specified complex discriminant and, after removing boundary/old-module
pieces, obtain a nonzero closed T5 period. Exact certificates or a controlled
nonzero numerical enclosure would establish survival; a bare nonzero K3
period or an auxiliary telescoper does not. Extra density parameters may
produce a still larger new factor, so failure to detect precisely this T5
factor would not prove old-class closure.

## 7. A precise version of “ψ_n0 falls” and the cheapest ψ50 evidence

Let K be the algebraic angular coefficient field on a regular interior chart.
Let C3 be the differential tensor category generated by the old algebraic/Tate,
classical and elliptic modules through order three, allowing subquotients,
tensor products and successive integrations/extensions. Its pure nonabelian
reductive factors are of elliptic SL2 type. Let M_n be the differential module
generated by the *actual physical* ψ_n0. State three separate conjectures:

* **CONJ A — new class at four:** M_4 has a nonzero irreducible subquotient
  outside C3, specifically the transverse rank-five SO5 constituent if the
  proposed physical pairing survives. The auxiliary S_e2 result proves the
  candidate exists; survival remains OPEN.
* **CONJ B — propagation:** for n≥4, M_n continues to have a constituent
  outside C3. Persistence of the same rank-five piece would suffice. This
  does not assert that the rank increases at every order.
* **CONJ C — growth:** sup_n rank_K M_n=∞, or a stated unbounded family of
  new pure factors/extension lengths. This is stronger than A or B. New log
  weights, a single K3, or persistence into ψ50 do not prove it.

None is a claim that a fixed ψ_n0 has infinite differential rank. The earlier
conditional fixed-order holonomic direct-image argument remains compatible
with all three conjectures. A bounded universal function class or bounded
all-order rank can fail while every fixed-order closure is finite.

There is a useful **PROVED conditional propagation shortcut**. In the current
Fock convention the order-five log-free source is

    L5 ψ50 = 14 ψ51 + 2 ψ52 - 2 V ψ40 + 2E ψ30,
    L5=Λ²-45.

The coefficient 14 and the ψ52 term must not be dropped. This odd-order
singlet inverse is nonresonant: its S3 frequency is (5+2)/2=7/2. Suppose A has
been established and the full right-hand side has a nonzero quotient outside
C3 after the indicated cancellations. Since C3 is closed under rational
angular differentiation, L5 could not send a member of C3 to that source.
Therefore ψ50 also lies outside C3. **No new Green integration is needed for
that implication.** Algebraic nonzero multiplication by V is invertible on a
regular chart, so it preserves the offending module up to algebraic gauge;
the issue is cancellation against the other source terms.

The cheapest next evidence is therefore: derive/project ψ51 and ψ52 as far
as needed, retain the actual ψ40 constituent, and show

    projection_new(14 ψ51+2 ψ52-2V ψ40+2E ψ30) != 0.

If ψ51, ψ52 and ψ30 are certified in C3, nonzero projection of -2Vψ40 is enough.
Their membership is a hypothesis to check, not assumed here. If they carry
new components, compute only their cancellation in this quotient. This
establishes propagation to five, not unbounded growth. For six use the full
analogous source and its even-order compatibility/projection; no all-order
noncancellation or rank-growth theorem is presently proved.
