2. Weak form, assembly and quadrature

Independently transforming the physical distance measure gives
s^5 (1-x²)y/8 ds dx dy, up to the common orientation constant. The kinetic metric
in (r1,r2,r12) has g11=g22=1, g33=2,
g13=(r1²+r12²-r2²)/(2r1 r12), g23=(r2²+r12²-r1²)/(2r2 r12).
Multiplying by that measure gives the seven implemented kinetic weights, with
the overall kinetic factor 1/2. The potential weight is -Z*y/2+(1-x²)/8.
The implemented derivative chains, including the derivative of exp(-eps*s),
are correct. Exact symbolic checks and ten independent Gamma derivatives through
log power four pass (sync4_weakform.py/.out). The half-triangle factor two is
appropriate for exchange-even trial states; atom x derivatives are correctly
combined before folding. No extra factor is missing from the Duffy Jacobian y.

Moment-table assembly applies product-rule atom derivatives and uses radial
Gamma/Bell moments. Mirroring H and S enforces matrix symmetry; symmetry alone
cannot test derivatives, quadrature, or the form domain. The zeta(3) series
converges rapidly enough for these working precisions. The constant Fock-log
replacement changes the polynomial trial space rather than enlarging it; the
small-N energy worsening is expected and visible. The atoms2 space contains the
log2 space at a fixed N, and adds chi and log(R/s) trial terms. These finite
polynomial/log/physical-chi combinations have integrable corner and collision
behavior and exponential tails, consistent with form-domain Ritz use.

The tanh–sinh nodes drop boundary regions at 1e-28 and angular differences below
1e-50. The polynomial moment test is useful but does not bound all atom-derivative
moments. Gram condition estimates reach about 5.61e48 at N=11; entrywise accuracy,
linear solve residuals, and discretization error must be distinguished. Raising
BigFloat precision controls roundoff, not quadrature or atom approximation.
No rigorous enclosure is produced by the code or this review.

Independent driver sync4_ritz.jl reads Claude's assembly without writing its files,
sets precision BEFORE including constants, refines tanh–sinh from h=1/16 to 1/24
and 1/32, extends the tail to 5, and checks Cholesky and generalized residuals.
The original driver sets precision after inclusion (some constants have default
256-bit accuracy); that is not the limiting uncertainty of these results.
Refined N=11 errors are 2.4314823228526578e-17 (384 bits, h=1/24)
and 2.4314823230015396e-17 (512 bits, h=1/32), differing by 1.49e-27.
Both Gram Cholesky checks pass; scaled residuals are about 1.2851e-50.
Detailed refined results are in sync4_ritz24.out and sync4_ritz32.out.

3. Independent a21 finite-part estimator

sync4_a21.jl includes no Claude source. It reimplements chi from the explicit
Clausen formula and extends Codex's radial Mellin functional to the exported
(n,p,a,b,atom,coef) states. All atoms2_N2 through atoms2_N11 were evaluated with
angular orders 24,48,96 at 192 bits, with N=10,11 repeated at 288 bits. The source
uses each exported variational E; the radius/log conversion and finite parts
at n=0,1 are retained. A manufactured radial finite-part check independently
recovers the -b/8 constant. The angular projection normalization is one to about
2e-15; angular nodes/weights are Float64, so high BigFloat arithmetic does not
supply arbitrary quadrature precision.

4. psi30 / atoms4

The Float64 psi30 branch clamps alpha/theta to 1e-12 inside boundaries, uses
Float64 values and AD gradients for a numerically integrated atom, and zeroes
non-finite gradients. The resulting value and derivative tables are not proven
to describe one differentiable trial function. Small node weights do not alone
bound errors in a severely conditioned pencil. These approximations, rather
than a failure of the variational principle, are a plausible explanation of the
N>=8 violation. A definitive attribution to a particular implementation defect
would require a consistent high-precision value/derivative and quadrature audit.
The source also supplies E0 and a21 to psi30: those atoms are informed by those
known constants and cannot independently determine them.

DISSENT from treating positive atoms4 errors at N<=7 as certified results.
They are exploratory evidence: positivity alone cannot certify consistency, and
N=7's 2.7e-14 gain lies in a range potentially sensitive to Float64 integration/AD.
Exclude N>=8 from physical convergence comparisons; do not interpret the N=10
positive error as recovery of a valid sequence after the preceding violations.
The chi/lR atoms2 calculations do not use this Float64 psi30 branch.

5. Convergence rate and minimality

At N=11 the saved errors are polynomial 1.4873e-7 (203 functions), log1
1.4062e-11 (357), log2 1.5746e-12 (469), atoms2 2.4315e-17 (713).
Equal-N comparisons use different numbers of functions. At roughly comparable
sizes, atoms2 N=9 (404) gives 3.8700e-15 versus log1 N=11 (357) 1.4062e-11;
atoms2 N=8 (289) gives 4.5909e-14 versus log2 N=9 (270) 7.5055e-11.
There is a substantial efficiency gain as well as an equal-N gain.

DISSENT from “faster than algebraic.” A finite table cannot establish this
asymptotic property. Fits of log(error) versus log(size) give slopes -7.74
(N=4..11), -7.91 (N=6..11), and -8.45 (N=8..11); the last adjacent effective
exponent is about 8.35. This is consistent with a higher algebraic order over
the observed range. No exponential/superalgebraic rate theorem follows.

The existing form-domain admissibility, representation gauges, boundary/branch
conditions, physical Gram matrices, and conditional form-core hypotheses remain
unchanged. Numerics do not establish a growing-block Pincherle theorem,
completeness, or continuity/error certification for a21. The dated update to
codex/phase2/codex_minimality2.md records the numerical evidence and these limits.
