\section{Direct verification of the Fock recurrence}\label{app:direct-recurrence}
This verification starts with the compact expression itself and the lower-order coefficients in \eqref{eq:p20}--\eqref{eq:p31}.
It does not use a third-order formula from Langner's thesis.

Put $a=\cos\alpha$ and $v=\cos\beta=\sin\alpha\cos\theta$.
On functions of these two coordinates, the angular operator is
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda^2=-4\bigl[(1-a^2)\partial_a^2+(1-v^2)\partial_v^2
 -2av\partial_a\partial_v-3a\partial_a-3v\partial_v\bigr].
\end{equation}
This is $-4\Delta_{S^3}$ restricted to functions of the first two Cartesian coordinates of the unit three-sphere.
The physical interior is the disk $a^2+v^2<1$.

\subsection{The integral terms}
In each panel use $b$ and $y$ as in the compact formula and set
\begin{gather}
 P=1+b+(2-4y^2)t^2+(1-b)t^4,\qquad
 N=(1+t^2)\{1+b+(1-b)t^2\},\\
 L(t)=\log\frac{1+t}{1-t},\qquad
 \widehat A=1+\frac2\pi\arcsin\frac{2yt}{\sqrt N}.
\end{gather}
The operator in these coordinates is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D=-4(1-b^2)\partial_b^2-(1-y^2)\partial_y^2
 -\frac{2b(1-2y^2)}y\partial_b\partial_y
 +12b\partial_b-\frac{2-5y^2}{y}\partial_y-21.
\end{equation}
The two angular panels carry the overall factors $\sqrt2 Z$ and $\sqrt2 Z^2$; the rotated panels carry $Z^2$.
After removing these factors, their weights $Q=q_0+q_2t^2$ and their associated polynomials $H$ are
\begin{align}
 Q_{A1}&=\frac{b^2-1}{72}
 +\frac{(b-1)(-18b-32y^2+16)}{288}t^2,\\
 H_{A1}&=-\frac t{36}\{(18b+32y^2-25)t^2-4b-8\},\\
 Q_{A2}&=\frac{2-b^2}{64}
 +\frac{5(b-1)(2y^2-1)}{32}t^2,\\
 H_{A2}&=\frac t8\{(20y^2-10)t^2-b-5\},\\
 Q_{R2}&=\frac{1-b^2}{18}
 +\frac{2(b-1)(2y^2-1)}9t^2,\\
 H_{R2}&=\frac{4t}9\{(8y^2-4)t^2-b-2\}.
\end{align}
Direct differentiation gives, in all three cases,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:direct-panel-certificate}
 \mathcal D\!\left(\frac{Q\widehat A}{\sqrt P}\right)
 =\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\!\left(\frac{H\widehat A}{\sqrt P}\right)
 +\frac{\rho(t)}\pi,
 \qquad
 \rho(t)=\frac{c_1t}{1+t^2}+\frac{c_2t}{D}+\frac{c_3t}{D^2},\quad
 D=1+b+(1-b)t^2.
\end{equation}
The coefficients are
\begin{align*}
(c_1,c_2,c_3)_{A1}={}&\left(
 -\frac{22b+32y^2-17}{36y},
 \frac{(b-1)(14b+32y^2+1)}{36y},
 \frac{(b+1)(7b+16y^2-6)}{18y}\right),\\
(c_1,c_2,c_3)_{A2}={}&\left(
 \frac{b+20y^2-5}{8y},
 -\frac{(b-1)(-b+20y^2-5)}{8y},
 \frac{b^2-20by^2+10b-20y^2+8}{8y}\right),\\
(c_1,c_2,c_3)_{R2}={}&\left(
 \frac{4(b+8y^2-2)}{9y},
 -\frac{4(b-1)(-b+8y^2-2)}{9y},
 -\frac{4(b+1)(-b+8y^2-3)}{9y}\right).
\end{align*}
The accompanying certificate checks \eqref{eq:direct-panel-certificate} by exact polynomial arithmetic.
Writing $H=h_0t+h_2t^3$, integration by parts therefore gives
\begin{align}\label{eq:direct-panel-image}
 \mathcal D\int_0^1\frac{LQ\widehat A}{\sqrt P}\,\dd t
 ={}&2h_2K_\infty+2(h_0+h_2)K_1
 +(h_0+h_2)\frac{\widehat A(1)}{\sqrt{P(1)}}\log2\\
 &+\frac1\pi\left\{\frac{c_1\pi^2}{16}
 +\frac{c_2x^2}{4(1-b)}+\frac{c_3x}{4\sin x}\right\},\qquad b=\cos x.\nonumber
\end{align}
Here $K_1$ has the same finite-part convention as in the preceding reductions.
The finite endpoint term containing $\log2$ must be retained: it is what remains after cancellation of the two logarithmic divergences in the integration by parts.
All parameter differentiations are justified on compact subsets of the physical interior by an integrable logarithmic majorant.
Thus applying the Fock operator removes the elliptic integrals altogether.

\subsection{Cancellation of the residual}
Insert the proved classical expressions for $K_\infty$ and $K_1$ in \eqref{eq:direct-panel-image}, differentiate the classical part of the compact formula, and subtract the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:fullsource}.
The resulting expression contains only algebraic coefficients, angles, real logarithms, and Clausen functions.
The substitution $T=\tan(\alpha/4)$, $U=\tan(\beta/4)$ makes the algebraic coefficients rational over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, with the stated constants adjoined.
The identities $\mathrm{Cl}_2'(q)=-\log|2\sin(q/2)|$ and $(\log|2\sin q|)'=\cot q$ supply the derivatives.
Logarithm duplication and Clausen reflection put all terms in a common notation.
The certificate uses an algebraically regrouped implementation of the classical coefficient table; a separate exact comparison checks that regrouping, including the angle polynomial and remainder.
The certificate separately checks the coefficients of $1,Z,Z^2,Z^3,E,EZ,a_{21},Za_{21}$.
Every resulting rational numerator is zero.
This proves the recurrence identically throughout the physical interior, rather than only at sampled points.
The standalone verification is supplied in \texttt{verification/direct\_recurrence/}; running \texttt{verify\_all.py} regenerates the residuals and checks their exact zeros and their links to the coefficient tables.

\subsection{Regularity and uniqueness}
The integral part has a uniform bound even at the degenerate angular limits.
For the physical parameters, $y\leq0$ and $0\leq P\leq N$, so
\begin{equation}
 0\leq\frac{\widehat A}{\sqrt P}
 =\frac{2}{\pi\sqrt P}\arcsin\sqrt{\frac PN}
 \leq\frac1{\sqrt N}\leq\frac1{\sqrt2\,t}.
\end{equation}
The removable value is used when $P=0$.
Since $L(t)/t$ is integrable and the polynomial weights are bounded, dominated convergence gives bounded, continuous panel values on the closed disk.
For the classical part, the physical angles obey
$|\alpha-\pi/2|+|\beta-\pi/2|\leq\pi/2$.
With the shifts in \eqref{eq:classical-general}, every $\mathcal L$ argument lies in $[-\pi/2,3\pi/4]$, where $\mathcal L$ is continuous and its apparent singularity at zero is removable.
The two logarithm arguments lie in $[\pi/4,\pi/2]$ and $[\pi/2,3\pi/4]$, respectively, so their sines are bounded away from zero.
The complete candidate is therefore bounded and continuous.

The disk boundary lifts to the smooth circle $C=\{x_3=x_4=0\}\subset S^3$.
It is a removable set for this bounded solution.
For completeness, take smooth logarithmic cutoffs that vanish within distance $\varepsilon^2$ of $C$ and equal one beyond distance $\varepsilon$.
They can be chosen with both $\|\nabla\chi_\varepsilon\|_{L^1}$ and $\|\Delta\chi_\varepsilon\|_{L^1}$ tending to zero.
Testing the interior equation against $\chi_\varepsilon\varphi$, boundedness makes all cutoff derivative terms vanish in the limit.
The source lies in $L^2$, as established above, so the recurrence holds distributionally on the whole sphere.
Elliptic regularity gives an $H^2(S^3)$ solution.
Finally, $4n(n+2)\ne21$ for every nonnegative integer $n$, so the solution is unique.
This identifies the compact expression with $\psi_{3,0}$ directly from the recurrence and the specified lower-order coefficients.
