Direct recurrence verification: regularity and uniqueness

The angular operator is -4 Delta_{S^3} on functions of the first two Cartesian
coordinates (a,v) of S^3. The physical open disk is a^2+v^2<1. Its boundary
lifts to the smooth circle C={x3=x4=0} in S^3, not a boundary hypersurface.
The three binary collision points lie on this circle.

Uniform boundedness of the displayed compact formula
For each universal panel let N=(1+t^2)(1+b+(1-b)t^2), P=N-4y^2t^2.
Physical parameters have -1<=b<=1, y<=0, and P>=0. Hence, for 0<t<1,
 Ahat/sqrt(P) = (2/pi) asin(sqrt(P/N))/sqrt(P) <= 1/sqrt(N)
             <= 1/(sqrt(2) t).
The value at P=0 is interpreted by its removable limit. The last inequality
uses N>=2t^2(1+t^2)>=2t^2 for b in [-1,1]. Since
 log((1+t)/(1-t))/t is integrable on (0,1), and each polynomial weight Q is
uniformly bounded on the compact parameter set, every panel is uniformly
bounded. Dominated convergence gives its continuous extension to the closed
disk. This treats simultaneous parameter/end-point degenerations too.

For the classical part, physical angles satisfy
 |alpha-pi/2|+|beta-pi/2|<=pi/2.
Thus w_+=(alpha+beta-3pi/2)/4 in [-pi/4,0], and
w_-=(alpha-beta+pi/2)/4 in [0,pi/4]. All arguments w_s+m*pi/4,
m=-1,0,1, lie in [-pi/2,pi/2]. L(u)=int_0^u x cot(x) dx is continuous
there; the apparent singularity at u=0 is removable. T(w)=int_0^w x tan(x)
dx and log|2cos(w)| are bounded for |w|<=pi/4. The one-variable L arguments
also lie in [-pi/2,pi/2]. Polynomial coefficients and angle polynomials are
bounded. State-dependent algebraic terms are bounded. Therefore the complete
compact formula is bounded and continuous on S^3.

Removing the circle
Assume the exact residual identity has been checked on the open disk.
It then holds pointwise on S^3\C. To extend it distributionally choose a
smooth cutoff chi_e that is zero within distance e^2 of C, one beyond distance
e, and logarithmic between, with smooth endpoint transitions. In tube
coordinates about a codimension-two smooth circle, one may arrange
 ||grad chi_e||_L1 ->0 and ||Delta chi_e||_L1 ->0.
For the totally geodesic circle in S^3 the radial density is sin(r)cos(r),
so Delta log r=(cot r-tan r)/r-1/r^2=O(1); the main annulus contribution is
O(e^2/|log e|), and the smoothed endpoint transitions contribute O(1/|log e|).
For a smooth test phi apply the interior PDE to chi_e phi. All derivative-of-
cutoff terms tend to zero by the boundedness of the candidate. The source is
L2 (the already specified lower Fock coefficients are bounded and Coulomb
singularities are inverse distances to three points). Dominated convergence
then yields the full distributional equation on S^3.

Elliptic regularity gives F in H2. The eigenvalues of -4 Delta_{S^3} are
4n(n+2), n=0,1,2,...; none equals 21. Thus the equation has at most one H2
solution. This argument uses neither Langner's third-order formula nor a
numerical comparison to the Green integral. Its only contingent premise is
the exact interior residual certificate, which passed exactly in all eight sectors and is recorded
in certificate.json. It presumes the stated lower-order coefficients/conventions.
